# Inspiration-channel analysis — Logically Answered & ColdFusion

Snapshot: 2026-07-06. Source: YouTube Data API v3 (50 most-recent long-form uploads each).
Performance is measured as **views/day** (total views ÷ age in days) so recent uploads
are compared fairly against older ones. "×baseline" = a video's views/day vs its own
channel's median views/day.

## Baselines side by side

| Metric | Logically Answered | ColdFusion |
|---|--:|--:|
| Median views (recent 50) | **181K** | **714K** |
| Median views/day (age-adj.) | 1,965 | 3,339 |
| Typical length | ~15 min | ~20 min |
| Upload cadence | every ~3 days | every ~7 days |
| Median engagement (likes+comments/views) | 3.5% | 3.8% |
| Titles w/ a number | 10% | 19% |
| Titles referencing money | 6% | 6% |

**Read:** ColdFusion is the "prestige" model — ~4× the median views, longer, slower,
bigger evergreen swings. Logically Answered is the "velocity" model — half the length,
2×+ the cadence, and hits driven by *this week's* business drama. Both are viable
templates for Foreign Frauds; Logically Answered's is the more realistic starting point
for a young channel (cheaper per video, more at-bats, topical).

## What they do well (the repeatable levers)

1. **Downfall narratives.** The engine is schadenfreude — the mighty falling. Recurring
   words: *collapse, disaster, trouble, ruined, prison, "getting what they deserve."*
   Fraud is a native fit for this and both channels already dip into it (ColdFusion's
   "Another Forbes 30u30 Facing Prison" pulled 11.3K/day, 3.4× baseline).

2. **A named protagonist.** Every hit is *a specific company or person* — Upwork, Klarna,
   SpaceX, the Flappy Bird creator — never an abstract theme. Concreteness sells.

3. **Timeliness drives the spikes.** The very top performers on both channels are days old
   (Logically's "tokens are getting too expensive" = 92K/day at 3 days; ColdFusion's
   "AI Data Centres" = 46K/day at 28 days). Views/day rewards reacting inside the news cycle.
   The weakest videos are older, structural, evergreen-decline pieces (Exxon, DirecTV, BP).

4. **Curiosity-gap titles.** Logically leans on ellipses and incomplete sentences
   ("Uh oh...", "It's (Finally) Bursting...") and a reusable franchise template —
   **"X Is (Finally) Getting What They Deserve…"** appears repeatedly and reliably charts.
   ColdFusion uses cleaner declaratives ("SpaceX Went Public – A Disaster Waiting to Happen").
   Titles are short: median 37–43 characters.

5. **Consistency.** Tight length band and a predictable cadence — the audience knows the format.

## Implications for Foreign Frauds

- **Differentiator = the "foreign" angle.** Both inspirations skew US/Western tech &
  business. International / non-Western frauds are an under-served lane where Foreign Frauds
  can own the niche rather than compete head-on.
- **Money in the title is an edge, not a cliché.** Only 6% of either channel's titles cite a
  dollar figure — yet fraud stories come with built-in numbers. Foreign Frauds already does
  this ("$12 Billion Fake-Cash Fraud", "$300 Million in Fake Sales"); lean in — a concrete
  sum is a curiosity + stakes hook competitors mostly leave on the table.
- **Start on the Logically model:** ~12–15 min, higher cadence, named protagonist + downfall +
  a number. Graduate toward ColdFusion length/production as the channel builds.
- **Chase recency.** The single biggest performance lever is covering a fraud while it is
  still in the news. That is exactly what the next phase (web search for recent frauds) feeds.

## Baseline to beat (reference, not a first-video target)
A mature channel in this lane clears ~150–200K views/video (Logically) with the format above.
For a new channel the actionable targets are the *inputs* the data rewards: named subject,
downfall arc, dollar figure in the title, 12–15 min, and a topic that broke recently.
