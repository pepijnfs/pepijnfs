# @vagabliss — Shooting Field Guide (phone, beginner)

Practical shooting tips translated from the verified research. No gear snobbery — this is all
phone-doable. The goal: give your editor (or the AI auto-cutter) enough good, steady, varied
clips to build a reel.

## Phone settings — set once, forget
- **Record in 4K, 60fps** if your phone offers it (Settings → Camera → Record Video). Storage/heat
  issue? 4K 30 or 1080p 60 is totally fine. *(Verified: high bitrate matters more than resolution —
  your phone's highest setting is high-bitrate, so just use it.)*
- **Format:** if you can pick, set "Most Compatible" (H.264) over "High Efficiency" (HEVC) — Instagram
  likes H.264. Otherwise just re-export as H.264 when editing.
- **Grid ON** — keeps horizons level and helps you frame.
- **Shoot vertical (9:16)** for reels. Keep your subject in the **top two-thirds** — the bottom of the
  screen gets covered by the caption and buttons.

## The 3 things that separate amateur from pro footage
1. **Move slower than feels natural.** Walk heel-to-toe, phone in both hands, elbows tucked into your
   ribs. Shaky/fast movement is the #1 amateur tell.
2. **One move per shot.** Either a slow pan, OR a slow walk-forward (push-in), OR a reveal — never all
   at once. Pick one and do it smoothly.
3. **Hold each shot 5+ seconds** even if it feels too long. The editor cuts it down to ~1.5s on the
   beat — but only if you gave it enough to work with.

## Lock focus & exposure (stops flicker/refocus mid-shot)
- Tap and **hold** your subject until it says **AE/AF Lock**.
- Then slide the little sun icon up/down to set brightness before you record.

## Slow-motion & shutter (the honest beginner version)
- Don't stress the "180° shutter rule" — the stock camera won't let you set it. It only matters if you
  use a manual app (Blackmagic Camera is free) and want that cinematic look later.
- Want dreamy slow-mo of water or walking? Use your phone's **Slo-mo mode** — but *check it looks
  sharp*, because slow-mo secretly drops resolution on many phones. *(Verified.)*

## Stabilization without a gimbal
- **Brace** against a railing, wall, or tree for static shots.
- Two hands, elbows into your body, move from your hips not your wrists.
- **DIY glidecam:** rubber-band your phone to a mini tripod and hold the tripod legs low — the weight
  below your hand smooths out the bob. *(Verified trick.)*
- A €80 gimbal is the single best upgrade when you're ready.

## The shot menu — grab ALL of these at every location
Variety is what makes a montage work. Aim for **8–12 clips per place** (you'll use 6–8):
- **WIDE / establishing** — the whole scene. "Where are we?"
- **DETAIL / close-up** — texture: flowers, water, a coffee, a sign, a door handle.
- **POV** — what *you* see: walking through a doorway, a hand opening a gate, feet on the path.
- **REVEAL** — start hidden (behind leaves/a wall), move to unveil the view.
- **MOTION** — something moving: a boat, water, curtains, you walking away.
- **THE STAY** ← your whole niche: the room, the bed, the balcony, breakfast, the view from the window.
  *Don't leave a hotel without these.*

## Light & timing
- **Golden hour** (first & last hour of daylight) = the "expensive" look. Shoot then when you can.
- Put the **sun behind or to the side** of your subject for a soft glow, not flat front-light.
- **Avoid harsh midday sun** (hard shadows, blown-out sky). Stuck at noon? Shoot in **open shade**.

## Get a human in it
Your brand is *you*, so land at least one human shot per location: walking away down a path, a hand
reaching for a door, sitting with a coffee. Prop the phone on a wall/bench, hit record, walk in.

## The mindset
**Overshoot.** Storage is cheap; you can't re-shoot a location you've left. Steady + slow + variety
beats fancy every time.
