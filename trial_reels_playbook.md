# Trial Reels: Feature Notes and Pre-Publish Checklist

Reference for testing reels on non-followers before they hit the grid. Two parts: the verified
feature behavior, and a creator's tactics (officialjonathankim) captured for evaluating our own
reels before publishing. Tactics are labeled where they are the creator's claim, not documented
Instagram behavior.

## The feature (verified, Instagram official)

- A trial reel publishes to non-followers only. Followers do not see it in feed or the Reels tab.
- It does not appear on the grid or profile Reels tab unless you tap "Share to Everyone."
- Non-followers are the growth audience, so a trial is a pure reach and discovery test.
- Insights arrive within 24 hours: views, likes, comments, shares.
- Eligibility: public account, 1,000+ followers.
- After the test: tap "Share to Everyone" manually, or enable auto-share (Instagram pushes it to
  followers if it gains traction within the first 72 hours).

Source: Instagram for Creators (creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels).

## Creator tactics: officialjonathankim (his claims)

Captured as-is for evaluation. Not all of it is verified.

1. **Keep a trial reel as a trial reel.** Do not move it to the main page. He claims moving it
   hurts the view ceiling, that trial reels accumulate views over time, and to keep it a trial for
   up to 6 months. [Unverified claim. Instagram documents test-then-share, not a 6-month
   accumulation or a view-ceiling penalty for sharing.]

2. **Keep it simple.** What performs on trial reels differs from main-feed content. His best
   performers are simple 7-second videos with a strong text hook that pushes people to the caption.

3. **Write a strong text hook.** Formula: open with a pain point the audience actually feels, then
   position yourself as the person who already solved it.

4. **Drive people to the caption.** Put a secondary text line under the hook to move viewers into
   the caption. Put the actual solution in the caption as a list or a story, not in the text hook.

5. **Use a reference.** His own reel follows all of the above; he tells viewers to save it as a
   template.

## Caveat for our brand

His format is text-hook and pain-point driven, which is a growth/education style. Our lane is
cinematic travel, and we chose a textless aesthetic. For cold non-followers a short text hook does
aid comprehension in the first second, so trial reels are the one context where a small text hook
may earn its place. That is a per-reel decision, not a rule change.

## Pre-publish checklist (use before every reel)

Combines the verified best practices with the tactics above.

- [ ] Hook lands in the first second (strong opening shot, or a short text hook if testing cold).
- [ ] Simple and short. 7 to 15 seconds for a scenic or quick reel.
- [ ] One clear idea, not a data dump. Every shot is a keeper, no filler.
- [ ] Pacing around 1.5s per shot, cut on the beat. Not faster.
- [ ] Caption is keyword-led (town, region, country in the first line) for Instagram and Google search.
- [ ] Caption carries the value or the payoff (price, what to do, why it is worth it).
- [ ] One share CTA and one save CTA.
- [ ] 5 hashtags maximum, in the first comment at publish time.
- [ ] Native upload, H.264, 1080x1920, vertical, no other-platform watermark.
- [ ] Decide: publish normally, or run as a trial reel first to test on non-followers.

## How to use trial reels for us

1. For a new or uncertain reel, publish it as a trial to test on non-followers.
2. Read the 24-hour insights: views, then saves and shares.
3. If it performs, tap "Share to Everyone" (or use auto-share) so followers see it too.
4. If it underperforms, keep it off the grid and apply what the numbers show to the next one.
